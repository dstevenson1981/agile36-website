## Ambiguities and wording edits

The original stems and choices were retained in the local reviewed-questions.json artifact, except for the explicitly recorded option clarification for question 36. The code uses the clarified wording below. These edits avoid presenting a debatable original question as having a verified official answer.

### Photo question 14 (updated exam question 14)

Correction: B → D. The course explicitly lists identifying risks and dependencies for management review as a team breakout #1 best practice (lesson 3, slide 40). Writing Stories for the first two Iterations is useful planning work but is not the best match to this draft-plan-review question.

### Photo question 17 (updated exam question 17)

Correction: B → D. A hallucination is fabricated, incorrect, or nonsensical generated output; it is not a complete failure to operate on unfamiliar data. See lesson 4, slides 55–56.

### Photo question 21 (updated exam question 20)

Ambiguous original: IMG_0227 selects A; IMG_0228 selects B. Both stakeholder review and measuring integrated ART progress are real benefits. The updated stem explicitly asks about measuring ART progress, making B the single best answer. This is a wording clarification, not proof that the original A selection was incorrect.

Original: What is one benefit of holding regular system demos?

Updated: Which benefit of regular System Demos provides an objective measure of progress across the Agile Release Train?

### Photo question 34 (updated exam question 32)

Ambiguous original: C is selected, but D is the stronger interpretation of the explicit customer-feedback detail. The original does not establish fearlessness, and it can evoke both experimentation and feedback. This is an interpretation, not an official answer-key verification. The updated wording focuses on collecting feedback and uses D; it removes "minimally" to avoid implying that unmet quality standards are acceptable.

Original: Team A works collaboratively on new functionality for a customer application. The acceptance criteria have each been minimally met. Team A decides to release the functionality with a method for collecting direct customer feedback. Which of the following high-performing team characteristics is Team A demonstrating?

Updated: Team A works collaboratively on new functionality for a customer application. The acceptance criteria have been met. The team releases the functionality with a method for collecting direct customer feedback. Which high-performing team characteristic is most directly demonstrated by collecting this feedback?

### Photo question 36 (updated exam question 34)

Answer C retained. Photo wording "working and functional updates from the previous iteration review" was clarified to "working functionality completed during the Iteration" because the review demonstrates the current increment, not a repeat of the previous review.

### Photo question 38 (updated exam question 36)

Correction: D → B. Respect is one of the five Scrum values; Communication is not. The Scrum Guide lists Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage.

### Photo question 45 (updated exam question 42)

Ambiguous original: A and B are both sound practices. The course explicitly recommends giving different team members the opportunity to demo (lesson 4, slide 38). A is retained, and the stem is narrowed to planning the demo during Iteration Planning so B is not penalized as if it were generally bad practice.

Original: What is one recommended practice for Scrum Masters/Team Coaches when facilitating Iteration Review?

Updated: Which recommended practice helps Scrum Masters/Team Coaches plan the Iteration Review demo when the team is planning its Iteration?
