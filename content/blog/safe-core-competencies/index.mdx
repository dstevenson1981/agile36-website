**The Scaled Agile Framework defines seven core competencies of business agility: Lean-Agile Leadership, Team and Technical Agility, Agile Product Delivery, Enterprise Solution Delivery, Lean Portfolio Management, Organizational Agility, and Continuous Learning Culture.** Together they describe everything an organization must be good at to deliver value at scale — and they're the backbone of how [SAFe](/blog/what-is-safe-framework) structures its courses, assessments, and roadmap.

Here's each competency in plain English: what it means, why it exists, and where you learn it.

## The Seven Competencies at a Glance

| Competency | One-Line Meaning | Where It's Taught |
|------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Lean-Agile Leadership** | Leaders model and enable the change | [Leading SAFe](/courses/leading-safe) |
| **Team and Technical Agility** | Skilled teams building quality solutions | [SAFe for Teams](/courses/safe-for-teams), [SSM](/courses/scrum-master) |
| **Agile Product Delivery** | Customer-centric, continuous value delivery | [POPM](/courses/product-owner-manager), [APM](/courses/agile-product-management) |
| **Enterprise Solution Delivery** | Building the world's largest systems lean-ly | Advanced/architect tracks |
| **Lean Portfolio Management** | Strategy and funding aligned to execution | [LPM course](/courses/lean-portfolio-management) |
| **Organizational Agility** | The whole business adapts, not just IT | Leading SAFe, transformation work |
| **Continuous Learning Culture** | Everyone improves, relentlessly | Every role, every course |

## 1. Lean-Agile Leadership

The foundation competency. SAFe is explicit that transformations live or die on leadership behavior — leaders must understand the [Lean-Agile mindset](/blog/what-is-safe-framework), apply its principles in their own decisions, and actively lead the change rather than delegate it. This is the heart of the [Leading SAFe curriculum](/blog/leading-safe-course-curriculum).

## 2. Team and Technical Agility

The delivery engine: cross-functional Agile teams, organized into teams-of-teams on an [Agile Release Train](/blog/what-is-an-agile-release-train), applying built-in quality so speed doesn't rot the codebase. Team members earn it through [SAFe for Teams (SP)](/blog/what-is-a-safe-practitioner); team coaches through the [Scrum Master track](/blog/csm-vs-ssm-which-is-better).

## 3. Agile Product Delivery

Customer centricity plus cadence: design thinking to build the right thing, [PI Planning](/blog/what-is-pi-planning) to build it together, and DevOps with release on demand so the business ships when it chooses. Product Owners and Product Managers live here — it's the core of the POPM and [Agile Product Management](/courses/agile-product-management) certifications.

## 4. Enterprise Solution Delivery

The competency for truly big systems — hundreds of practitioners, hardware and software, compliance regimes. It extends the framework with practices for coordinating multiple ARTs and suppliers around a single solution. Most organizations meet it only after the earlier competencies are solid.

## 5. Lean Portfolio Management

Where strategy meets funding: portfolio vision tied to strategic themes, [value streams funded instead of projects](/blog/lean-portfolio-management), portfolio Kanban for visibility, and lean governance replacing stage gates. Portfolio leaders formalize it with the [LPM certification](/blog/safe-lpm-certification-cost-2026).

## 6. Organizational Agility

The competency that spreads agility beyond product development — lean-thinking people across the business, strategy that can change direction quickly, and business operations that flex with it. It's less a course and more the cumulative outcome of the transformation.

## 7. Continuous Learning Culture

The sustaining competency: a culture of relentless improvement, innovation, and learning — retrospectives that change things, Inspect & Adapt at the train level, and an organization that treats learning as work, not overhead.

## How the Competencies Are Used in Practice

**As a maturity map.** Scaled Agile's Measure and Grow assessments score an organization against each competency to show where the transformation actually stands — useful ammunition for prioritizing next steps.

**As a curriculum.** Every SAFe certification maps to competencies, which is why role selection matters: leaders need the full-spectrum view ([Leading SAFe](/courses/leading-safe)), teams need Team and Technical Agility ([SAFe for Teams](/courses/safe-for-teams)), portfolio leaders need LPM.

**As an exam blueprint.** SAFe certification exams draw questions competency by competency — knowing the seven cold is the cheapest exam prep there is. Start with our [Leading SAFe practice questions](/blog/leading-safe-exam-questions-2026).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Did SAFe always have seven competencies?**
No — earlier SAFe versions had five; the framework expanded to seven as it matured to cover the full enterprise (adding Organizational Agility and Continuous Learning Culture).

**Which competency should we build first?**
SAFe's own answer: Lean-Agile Leadership first (nothing survives without it), then Team and Technical Agility and Agile Product Delivery — the delivery core. Portfolio-level competencies come once trains are running.

**Are the competencies on the SAFe Agilist exam?**
Yes — the SA exam is organized around them. The [Leading SAFe curriculum](/blog/leading-safe-course-curriculum) walks all seven.
