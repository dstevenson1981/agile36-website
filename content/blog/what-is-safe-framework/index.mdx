# What is SAFe Framework?

SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework) is the world's leading framework for business agility. Scaled Agile's glossary defines it as an operating system that integrates Lean, Agile, and DevOps so an enterprise can deliver innovative products and services faster, more predictably, and with higher quality.

Scrum still fits one team. It does not, by itself, answer how strategy, funding, and delivery stay aligned when many teams share one solution. That is the problem SAFe is built for. The current public description is SAFe 6, not the older stack of four levels.

## How SAFe 6 is organized

SAFe is not four levels named Team, Program, Large Solution, and Portfolio. Scaled Agile's current public pages describe configurations and competencies, and the live glossary also names five disciplines.

Four configurations are still on the [Big Picture posters page](https://www.scaledagileframework.com/posters/) (last update printed on that page: 14 October 2025). The [glossary](https://scaledagileframework.com/glossary/) defines them the same way:

- **Essential SAFe** is the simplest starting point. It is the minimal set for Agile Release Trains to deliver solutions. The posters page calls it the most basic configuration.
- **Large Solution SAFe** is for enterprises building large and complex solutions that do not require portfolio concerns.
- **Portfolio SAFe** provides strategy and investment funding, Agile portfolio operations, and Lean governance for one or more value streams. The posters page describes it as multiple solutions with minimal dependencies, built by a modest number of Agile teams.
- **Full SAFe** is the most comprehensive configuration. The glossary says it supports enterprises that build and maintain a portfolio of large and complex solutions. The posters page says it supports large, integrated solutions that typically require hundreds of people or more to develop and maintain.

The glossary entry "SAFe Overview" calls the overview a visualization of the seven core competencies of business agility. These seven are defined on that same glossary page:

- Lean-Agile Leadership (LAL)
- Team and Technical Agility (TTA)
- Agile Product Delivery (APD). The entry's body now says Product Development Flow, formerly referred to across SAFe as Agile Product Delivery.
- Enterprise Solution Delivery (ESD). The entry's body now says Large Solution Integration and Delivery, formerly called Enterprise Solution Delivery.
- Lean Portfolio Management (LPM). The glossary defines LPM as aligning strategy and execution by applying Lean and systems thinking to strategy and investment funding, Agile portfolio operations, and governance.
- Organizational Agility (OA)
- Continuous Learning Culture (CLC)

The same glossary, under "SAFe Disciplines," says SAFe is organized into five disciplines: Leadership and Culture, Lean Portfolio Management, Large Solution Integration and Delivery, Product Development Flow, and Team and Technical Agility. The posters page lists those same five. The [framework home page](https://scaledagileframework.com/) points readers to the SAFe 6.0 Big Picture configurations and uses those discipline areas. Both the seven competencies and the five disciplines are on the live glossary.

The foundation, in the glossary, is anchored by Lean-Agile leadership and continuous learning culture. It holds the mindset, values, principles, and implementation guidance. There are ten Lean-Agile principles. There are four core values: alignment, transparency, respect for people, and relentless improvement.

An Agile Release Train is a long-lived team of Agile teams that incrementally develops, delivers, and often operates one or more solutions in a development value stream. An Agile team is a cross-functional group of typically ten or fewer people. PI Planning is a cadence-based event for the entire ART that aligns teams and stakeholders to a shared mission and vision. A Planning Interval (PI) is a cadence-based timebox in which Agile Release Trains deliver continuous value in alignment with PI objectives. Program Increment is the older name for that timebox.

Large solutions use a Solution Train when the work needs more than one ART and suppliers. Preparation for PI Planning across those trains is Pre-Plan. Lean Portfolio Management is a competency and a discipline. It is not a fourth level sitting on top of a program layer.

What makes SAFe effective is its emphasis on working software delivery. Unlike many enterprise frameworks that focus primarily on process, SAFe maintains Agile's commitment to frequent, predictable releases.

## Key SAFe Principles in Practice

SAFe operates on ten fundamental principles that guide decision-making at every level. The most impactful principle I've observed is "Assume variability; preserve options." This means architectural and business decisions should maintain flexibility rather than lock teams into specific approaches early in development.

Another critical principle is "Build incrementally with fast, integrated learning cycles." Enterprise software development often suffers from big-bang releases that fail spectacularly. SAFe emphasizes continuous integration, continuous deployment, and frequent customer feedback to reduce risk and improve outcomes.

The principle of "Base milestones on objective evaluation of working systems" fundamentally changes how enterprises measure progress. Instead of tracking completion percentages or deliverable counts, SAFe focuses on working software demonstrations and measurable business outcomes.

## Key Points

- **Agile Release Trains** synchronize teams through regular PI Planning events and shared objectives
- **Lean Portfolio Management** connects business strategy to development execution through value stream investment decisions
- **Working software focus** maintains Agile's emphasis on frequent, demonstrable releases despite enterprise complexity
- **Architectural runway** ensures teams can move fast without accumulating technical debt or creating integration problems
- **Continuous learning** through inspect-and-adapt workshops and communities of practice drives organizational improvement
- **Value delivery measurement** focuses on business outcomes rather than activity completion or resource utilization

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to implement SAFe in an enterprise?

Start with one Agile Release Train, show value, then expand. Essential SAFe is the simplest starting point. This page does not state a duration.

### What's the difference between SAFe and traditional Agile?

Traditional Agile (like Scrum) focuses on individual team effectiveness, while SAFe extends Agile principles to coordinate multiple teams working on shared solutions. SAFe adds PI Planning, Lean Portfolio Management, and the configuration you actually need, while preserving team-level Agile practices.
