**The core difference: Scrum organizes work into fixed-length sprints with defined roles and ceremonies, while Kanban manages continuous flow with work-in-process limits and no required roles or timeboxes.** Scrum asks "what can we commit to this sprint?"; Kanban asks "what should we finish next?"

Neither is "more agile" than the other — they solve different problems. Here's the practical comparison, when each wins, and why mature organizations (including every SAFe shop) usually end up using both.

## Kanban vs Scrum at a Glance

| Dimension | Scrum | Kanban |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Cadence** | Fixed sprints (usually 2 weeks) | Continuous flow |
| **Roles** | Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developers | None required — keep existing roles |
| **Commitment** | Sprint backlog, planned up front | Pull next item when capacity frees |
| **Key constraint** | Sprint timebox | WIP limits per workflow state |
| **Change mid-stream** | Discouraged within a sprint | Anytime — reprioritize the backlog |
| **Key metrics** | Velocity, sprint burndown | Cycle time, throughput, WIP |
| **Ceremonies** | Planning, daily scrum, review, retrospective | Optional — replenishment and delivery reviews as needed |
| **Best first fit** | Product teams building in increments | Support, ops, and interrupt-driven work |

For deeper dives on each side, see [What is Scrum?](/blog/what-is-scrum) and [What is Kanban?](/blog/what-is-kanban)

## When Scrum Is the Better Fit

**You're building a product in increments.** Sprints force prioritization, create a [demo-able increment](/blog/what-is-a-sprint-in-agile) every cycle, and give stakeholders a predictable rhythm.

**The team needs structure to change.** Scrum's defined roles and ceremonies give newly-agile teams a complete operating system rather than a blank page. That's also why it dominates job postings — see [how to become a Scrum Master](/blog/how-to-become-a-scrum-master-2026).

**Estimation and forecasting matter.** [Story points](/blog/what-is-story-points-in-agile) and velocity give planning traction that pure flow metrics take longer to provide.

## When Kanban Is the Better Fit

**Work arrives unpredictably.** Support queues, operations, maintenance — anywhere "plan two weeks ahead" collides with reality daily. Kanban meets the work as it comes.

**Handoffs and bottlenecks are the problem.** Visualizing every workflow state and capping WIP exposes exactly where work piles up — the fastest cure for a team that starts everything and finishes nothing.

**The team is experienced and stable.** Mature teams often outgrow sprint overhead; Kanban keeps the discipline (limits, metrics, explicit policies) while dropping ceremony weight.

## The Trick Question: Most Teams Use Both

The framing "Kanban **vs** Scrum" suggests you must pick one. In practice, the most common mature setup is a blend:

- **Scrum teams add Kanban boards and WIP limits** to manage flow inside the sprint ("Scrumban")
- **Kanban teams adopt retrospectives and reviews** from Scrum's ceremony kit
- **SAFe teams explicitly combine them** — the [Scaled Agile Framework](/blog/what-is-safe-framework) has every team plan on a common cadence (iterations and [PI Planning](/blog/what-is-pi-planning)) while applying Team Kanban with WIP limits to manage flow. SAFe's official team course teaches Scrum, Kanban, and XP practices together — that's the [SAFe for Teams (SP) certification](/courses/safe-for-teams).

So the real question isn't which framework wins — it's which constraint your team needs first: the **timebox** (Scrum) or the **WIP limit** (Kanban).

## How to Choose: Three Questions

1. **Is your work plannable two weeks out?** Yes → Scrum. No → Kanban.
2. **Does the team need role clarity and structure?** Yes → Scrum first, add Kanban later.
3. **Is the pain unfinished work and bottlenecks?** Start with Kanban's WIP limits — you can add them to any process today.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Kanban agile?**
Yes. Kanban implements agile principles — visualize work, limit WIP, optimize flow — without Scrum's roles or timeboxes. Agile is the mindset; Scrum and Kanban are two ways to practice it. See [Agile vs Waterfall](/blog/agile-vs-waterfall-2026) for the bigger picture.

**Can you use Kanban inside Scrum?**
Absolutely — a Kanban board with WIP limits inside a sprint is one of the most effective upgrades a Scrum team can make, and it's how SAFe teams operate by default.

**Which should I learn for my career?**
Learn both — they're complementary, and enterprise roles expect fluency in each. Scrum-path certifications ([CSM vs SSM](/blog/csm-vs-ssm-which-is-better)) suit facilitator roles; team members in SAFe organizations get both in one course with [SAFe for Teams](/courses/safe-for-teams).

**Is there a Kanban certification?**
Kanban University and others offer them, but most enterprise demand centers on Scrum and SAFe credentials that include Kanban practice — a practical reason many professionals start there.
