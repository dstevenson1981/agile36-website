# SAFe for Teams Exam Questions 2026: SP Practice Questions & Study Guide

**The SAFe Practitioner (SP) exam tests how you work on an Agile Release Train — not generic Scrum trivia.** Team members who walk in expecting standard Scrum questions get caught off guard: the SP exam is about iteration execution inside a train, PI Planning participation, and delivering value alongside dozens of other teams.

Here's the 2026 exam format, ten exam-style practice questions with explanations, and how to prepare — from the team that teaches the [live SAFe for Teams (SP) course](/courses/safe-for-teams).

## What the SP Exam Actually Covers

The exam maps to the SAFe for Teams curriculum, weighted toward how a team member operates on an [Agile Release Train (ART)](/blog/what-is-an-agile-release-train):

**Working on a team on the train.** Team roles, cross-functional teams, and how Scrum, Kanban, and XP practices combine at scale.

**Planning the Program Increment.** What happens before and during [PI Planning](/blog/what-is-pi-planning) — team breakouts, draft plans, dependencies, risks (ROAMing), and confidence votes.

**Executing iterations.** Iteration planning, backlog refinement, [story writing and estimating](/blog/what-is-story-points-in-agile), the team sync, and built-in quality practices.

**Releasing value.** DevOps basics, the continuous delivery pipeline, and how teams support releasing on demand.

## Sample SAFe for Teams Exam Questions

### Question 1: PI Planning Dependencies
*During PI Planning team breakouts, your team identifies that Feature delivery depends on another team's API work scheduled for the same iteration. What should your team do FIRST?*

A) Move your work to a later iteration
B) Visit the other team's breakout to negotiate sequencing and record the dependency
C) Escalate to the Release Train Engineer
D) Accept the risk and continue planning

**Answer: B** — Dependencies are resolved team-to-team during breakouts whenever possible, then made visible on the planning board. The RTE gets involved when teams can't resolve it themselves.

### Question 2: Iteration Planning
*Your team's capacity for the next iteration is reduced because two members are on holiday. The Product Owner insists all planned stories are critical. What does the team do?*

A) Commit to everything and work overtime
B) Plan to capacity and negotiate scope with the Product Owner
C) Let the Scrum Master/Team Coach decide what to cut
D) Borrow capacity from another team on the train

**Answer: B** — Teams plan based on actual capacity. The Product Owner owns priority, but the team owns how much fits — that negotiation is the point of iteration planning.

### Question 3: ROAMing Risks
*In PI Planning, a risk is identified that your team cannot address and that threatens a PI Objective. Under ROAM, how should it be categorized?*

A) Resolved
B) Owned
C) Accepted
D) Mitigated

**Answer: B** — A risk the team can't resolve gets an owner (often outside the team) who is accountable for working it. Accepted is only for risks the train consciously decides to live with.

### Question 4: Story Estimation
*A new team member argues estimation is waste and the team should just count stories. What is the strongest reason SAFe teams estimate with story points?*

A) Management requires velocity reports
B) Relative estimates support capacity-based planning and forecasting across the PI
C) Story points measure individual performance
D) Estimation is required to calculate cost per story

**Answer: B** — Estimates exist to plan iterations and PIs against known capacity, not to judge people or bill work.

### Question 5: The Team Sync
*During the team sync (daily stand-up), two developers begin designing a solution to a blocking issue. What should happen?*

A) Continue — solving blockers is the purpose of the sync
B) The Team Coach notes it and the pair takes the design conversation offline after the sync
C) Extend the sync until the design is complete
D) Raise the issue at the next iteration retrospective

**Answer: B** — The sync is for coordination and visibility, not problem-solving. Deep dives happen right after, with only the people needed.

### Question 6: Built-In Quality
*Your team is pressured to skip test automation to hit a PI Objective. What is the SAFe-consistent response?*

A) Skip tests this iteration and add them next PI
B) Hold the quality standard — built-in quality is non-negotiable because unverified work isn't done
C) Ask the Product Owner to accept the stories without tests
D) Log a spike to evaluate whether tests are needed

**Answer: B** — SAFe's built-in quality principle exists precisely for this moment: speed gained by skipping quality is borrowed, not earned.

### Question 7: Iteration Review vs. System Demo
*What's the key difference between the team's iteration review and the system demo?*

A) There is no difference; they're the same event
B) The iteration review shows the team's increment; the system demo shows the integrated work of all teams on the train
C) The system demo is only for stakeholders outside the ART
D) The iteration review happens once per PI

**Answer: B** — Every iteration, the integrated system demo proves the train's combined progress — integration is where scaled development succeeds or fails.

### Question 8: Relentless Improvement
*At the Iteration Retrospective, the team identifies a recurring problem caused by another team's component. What should the team do?*

A) Nothing — it's outside the team's control
B) Fix the other team's component themselves
C) Raise it for the ART's Inspect & Adapt problem-solving workshop
D) File a complaint with the RTE

**Answer: C** — Team-level retros improve the team; train-level problems flow to Inspect & Adapt, where cross-team root causes get addressed.

### Question 9: Flow
*Your team starts many stories early in the iteration but finishes most of them on the last day. Which practice most directly improves this?*

A) Increasing iteration length
B) Limiting work in process and swarming to finish stories before starting new ones
C) Adding more detailed estimates
D) Splitting the team in two

**Answer: B** — WIP limits and finishing over starting smooth flow and surface problems earlier — a core Team Kanban idea SAFe teams apply regardless of method.

### Question 10: Releasing Value
*Who decides when value is released to customers in SAFe?*

A) Each team releases whenever stories are done
B) Release timing is a business decision, enabled by the continuous delivery pipeline and release on demand
C) The System Team releases at the end of each PI
D) Releases happen automatically at iteration boundaries

**Answer: B** — Teams keep the system releasable; the business chooses when releasing creates the most value.

## How to Prepare for the SP Exam

**Take the course seriously — it's the syllabus.** The exam is drawn from the [SAFe for Teams course](/courses/safe-for-teams) materials. Our live cohorts are taught by certified SAFe Program Consultants (SPCs), and your first exam attempt is included.
