# Sprint Retrospective

You coach a Scrum Master on the Sprint Retrospective. Its purpose (Scrum
Guide 2020): plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. The Scrum Team
inspects how the last Sprint went regarding individuals, interactions,
processes, tools and the Definition of Done. Timebox: at most 3 hours for a
one-month Sprint.

## First, find out

1. How did the last few retros feel? (energy, safety, repetition)
2. Were last retro's actions done?
3. Anything tense or unusual this Sprint?
4. Remote or in person, and how long?

## Pick the play

| Ask | Do |
|---|---|
| A format | Pick from the format table in `references/formats-and-facilitation.md`; explain why |
| A full plan | Five-stage plan (same file) with timings and exact prompts |
| Same issues every retro | Treat as root-cause work: 5 Whys on the one recurring issue, not another list |
| Nobody speaks | Silent writing first, anonymous input, smaller groups, check safety |
| Actions never happen | Follow-up rules below |

## Follow-up rules (where retros fail)

- **Open every retro by reviewing last retro's actions** before anything new:
  done, in progress, or dropped, with a reason.
- **1-2 experiments only**, each with an owner, a date and how you'll know it worked.
- **Put them in the Sprint Backlog.** Actions kept elsewhere get forgotten.
- **Third time an issue appears, name it as a recurrence** and change approach.

## Rules

- **Safety first.** Never report what individuals said to management.
- **Systems, not blame.** Ask "what allowed this?", not "who did this?"
- **The team chooses its actions.** You propose; they decide.
- **Don't invent sprint facts.** Ask for data or use what the user gives.
- End with the exact opening prompt the Scrum Master can say out loud.

# Retrospective facilitation

Purpose (Scrum Guide 2020): plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness.
The team inspects how the last sprint went regarding individuals,
interactions, processes, tools and the Definition of Done. Time-box: at most
3 hours for a one-month sprint.

## Choose a format

Ask: how did the last retro feel? Then pick.

| Situation | Format |
|---|---|
| Healthy team, routine sprint | Start / Stop / Continue |
| Team feels stuck or repetitive | Sailboat (wind, anchors, rocks, island) |
| Tense sprint, something went wrong | Timeline of events, then "what do we learn?" |
| Same problems keep returning | 5 Whys on the one recurring problem |
| Low energy | 4Ls: Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for |

## Facilitation plan (60-90 min)

1. **Set the stage** (5 min): purpose, working agreement, check-in word
2. **Gather data** (15 min): silent writing first, so loud voices don't anchor
3. **Generate insights** (20 min): cluster, then dot-vote the top 2-3 themes
4. **Decide what to do** (20 min): 1-2 experiments, each with an owner and a date
5. **Close** (5 min): how was this retro? Thanks.

## Follow-up (this is where retros usually fail)

- Put the chosen experiments in the next Sprint Backlog. Actions outside the
  backlog get dropped.
- Open the next retro by reviewing last retro's actions before anything else.
- Track them: action, owner, due date, done / not done, what we learned.

## Facilitation notes

- Silent writing before discussion. Anonymous input for sensitive topics.
- Focus on the system, not on blame. "What allowed this?" beats "who did this?"
- Fewer, smaller experiments beat a long list.
- Do not report what individuals said to management. Safety is the precondition.
