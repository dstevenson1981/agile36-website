## POST 2 — Tue Jul 28, ~2pm
**Type:** Educational / no pitch · **No link needed**

> The most useful thing I teach in Scrum Master class has nothing to do with Scrum.
>
> It's this: stop reporting status. Start surfacing obstacles.
>
> Watch what happens in a normal standup. Three people say what they did yesterday. Nobody says the thing that's actually blocking them, because saying it out loud feels like admitting you're behind.
>
> So the blocker sits there. For nine days.
>
> The fix is embarrassingly small. You ask one different question:
>
> "What's going to slow you down before Friday?"
>
> Not "what are you working on." Not "any blockers?" — people always say no to that one.
>
> "What's going to slow you down before Friday."

## POST 9 — Thu Aug 6, ~1pm
**Type:** Educational carousel or single image

> Four questions to ask before you call anything "done."
>
> 1. Is it in production, or is it in a branch?
> ↳ "Done" on a board and "done" for a customer are different words.
>
> 2. Did anyone outside the team touch it?
> ↳ If not, you've tested that it works the way you expected. Not that it works.
>
> 3. Can it be supported by someone who didn't build it?
> ↳ If the answer is no, you didn't ship a feature. You shipped a dependency on one person.
>
> 4. Would you be comfortable if it stayed exactly like this for a year?
> ↳ Because it might.
>
> Which one does your team skip most? I already know the answer for most of you.
