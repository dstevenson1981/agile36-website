## Why it worked

1. It challenges a professional identity using familiar activities readers recognize in their own work.
2. It creates accusation and then absolution. The reader feels challenged, but the post reframes the system rather than calling the reader incompetent.
3. Its three-part structure is easy to scan: what the job is not, what AI accelerated, and what remains essential.
4. The AI angle makes an evergreen role-definition debate timely.
5. Concrete lists make the post useful enough to save and easy to debate item by item.
6. The closing observation gives readers a compact idea to repeat: agents need context, and product leaders already lead through context.
7. Free resources appear after the value, so the body does not read as an advertisement.

The author's established audience also contributed. The engagement should not be attributed to copy structure alone.

## Improvement opportunity

The competitor post defines the role well but stops short of giving the reader a sharper operating principle. An original Agile36 version can improve it by focusing on the practical risk of AI speed: teams can produce more while learning nothing. The payoff should contrast output with learning and end with a question that invites real experience rather than generic agreement.
