# Original Agile36 rewrite

Competitor reference: Paweł Huryn. This draft uses the identity-correction mechanism but does not copy the competitor's language, list, sequence, or resources.

AI made product work faster.

It did not make weak product management better.

AI can now:

- Turn customer interviews into themes
- Query product data without waiting for a dashboard
- Build a rough prototype in one sitting
- Draft release notes before the meeting ends

Useful? Absolutely.

But speed has a cost when the thinking is weak.

It helps teams ship untested assumptions faster.

The product leader's job is not to keep the machine busy. It is to decide where uncertainty is most dangerous.

Before a team builds, someone still has to:

- Separate a stated request from the real problem
- Connect customer pain to business value
- Name the riskiest assumption
- Decide what evidence would change the plan
- Align product, design, and engineering around the trade-off
- Own the result after release

AI agents can research, synthesize, prototype, and monitor.

They still need context, boundaries, and evaluation.

Without those, they do not scale product judgment.

They scale product debt.

The advantage will not go to the PM using the most AI tools.

It will go to the PM who turns faster output into faster learning.

What did AI help your team learn this week, not just produce?
