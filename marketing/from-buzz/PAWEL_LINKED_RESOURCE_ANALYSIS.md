## Why the package worked

The LinkedIn post is an identity correction: it names familiar PM activities, removes their status as the core job, then restores a more strategic identity. This creates tension without humiliating the reader. The three-part structure is easy to scan, the AI angle makes an evergreen debate timely, and every bullet gives readers something to agree or argue with.

The three linked resources turn the post into a depth ladder:

1. The discovery guide answers what product managers should do when shipping becomes cheap.
2. The AI PM guide answers what the role now requires.
3. The agent framework answers how to lead autonomous systems with objectives, boundaries, and stop conditions.

Together they cover practice, identity, and implementation. They also demonstrate depth behind the short LinkedIn claim, increasing saves, clicks, and perceived authority. The author's established audience contributed to distribution, so the copy is not the only cause.

## Original replacement strategy

Do not mirror the competitor's titles, eight-part framework, examples, article order, or language. Build an Agile36 package around a distinct operating principle: AI lowers the cost of production, so product leaders must raise the quality of decisions.

Original assets:

1. **Discovery at AI Speed**: a risk-based decision loop for choosing whether to interview, prototype, pilot, or ship behind a flag.
2. **What AI Product Leaders Actually Own**: six responsibilities covering outcomes, failure definitions, evidence, evaluation, human handoffs, and guarded rollout.
3. **The Agent Delegation Contract**: a five-part operating agreement covering job, evidence, context, authority, and review.

These resources solve the same audience problems through different frameworks and language.
